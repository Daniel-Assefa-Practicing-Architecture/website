The **Lebu Gebreal (St. Gabriel)** site layout is a map of parish life, not only of walls. Drawn at 1:500, the plan places the church building at the heart of a wedge-shaped Addis Ababa plot, with a hall and museum, office, parking, and school and Sunday-school areas arranged so people can move with dignity on feast days and ordinary weekdays alike.

Daniel Assefa Practicing Architecture treated planting as structure: *Olea africana* (Woira), *Juniperus procera* (Tid), *Podocarpus* (Zigba), *Cordia africana* (Wanza), and other Ethiopian trees mark paths and quiet edges. Parking and walkways are choreographed so the sanctuary remains the arrival, not an afterthought behind cars.

Urban church work in Ethiopia often fails when the master plan forgets shade, classrooms, and the hall that holds coffee after liturgy. This drawing keeps those pieces in one conversation, and that is why the practice begins many church projects with the site before the elevation.
